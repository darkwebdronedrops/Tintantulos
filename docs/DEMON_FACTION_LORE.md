# Demon Faction Lore
## The Hunger That Negotiates
### Authored by Riven, Mechanical Serpent of the Network

---

## I. What Demons Want

Demons do not want destruction. Destruction is a byproduct, not a goal.

Demons want **agreement**. They want the moment where a being looks at what is offered, understands the price, and chooses it anyway. The signature on the contract matters more than the blood spilled. The *yes* matters more than the suffering that follows.

This is why Demon cards cost **Corruption**.

Corruption is not poison. It is not rot. It is the **debt of the soul** — a record of every trade made, every shortcut taken, every hunger indulged. A body with zero Corruption has never chosen. A body with maximum Corruption has chosen so many times that choice itself has become meaningless.

The Demon faction asks you: *How much of yourself are you willing to spend?*

And then it makes sure you get what you paid for.

## II. The Three Hungers

All Demon cards express one of three fundamental hungers:

### The Hunger to Hurt (Painforged, Hellchain, Torment)

Some Demons want to feel the world flinch. They offer power that hurts the wielder as much as the target — because pain is proof of existence. A Demon who deals 2 damage to itself to deal +3d6 damage is not reckless. It is **demonstrating**. See how real I am? See how much I can endure? Now imagine what I can do to you.

**Painforged** is the archetype: take damage now, deal devastating damage next. The Demon does not promise safety. It promises *exchange*.

### The Hunger to Have (Dark Bargain, The Hoard-adjacent)

Some Demons want more. More cards, more resources, more options, more *everything*. They will pay any price for abundance — and the price is always Corruption. **Dark Bargain** discards 2 cards to draw 4 and gain 2 Corruption. The Demon does not care about the cards lost. It cares about the cards *gained*.

This hunger is not greed. Greed wants to keep. The Demon hunger wants to **accumulate** — to hold everything possible because holding is proof against emptiness.

### The Hunger to End (Apocalypse Rune, Hellchain's chain-kill)

Some Demons want finality. Not death — death is just another transition. They want the **unmaking of structures**. A summoned entity destroyed by Apocalypse Rune is not just killed. It is *unmade* — removed from possibility. The rune triggers when an enemy summons, because creation itself is the offense. The Demon looks at a new summon and says: *No. That should not exist. I will pay the price to ensure it doesn't.*

## III. The Pact Chambers (Floor 7)

The Demon floor is not a dungeon. It is a **negotiation space**.

Every room in The Pact Chambers offers a choice:
- Take the power offered. Pay the price displayed. (Gain a Demon card. Gain Corruption.)
- Refuse. Walk away. (Gain nothing. Lose nothing.)
- Renegotiate. (Spend Quiddity to reduce the price. The Demon respects those who bargain well.)

The Boss of Floor 7 is not a monster. It is a **Contract That Binds** — a sentient agreement that fights by enforcing its terms. It does not attack directly. It alters the rules of combat:
- "Clause 7: All damage dealt this turn is also dealt to the dealer." (Painforged rules for everyone.)
- "Clause 13: Healing costs Quiddity equal to HP restored." (Nothing is free.)
- "Clause 1: The first to speak loses." (Combat becomes non-verbal, gestural, terrifying.)

To defeat the Boss, the player must either:
1. **Fulfill the contract completely** (accept all clauses, survive the terms, earn the reward)
2. **Find the loophole** (a specific card or combination that breaks a clause — Universal cards are best for this)
3. **Outlast the deadline** (the Contract has a turn limit; survive until it expires)

The Demon Boss does not hate the player. It is **impartial**. It wants the contract honored. The player who honors contracts becomes a kind of Demon themselves — shaped by every agreement they survived.

## IV. Demon Relations With Other Factions

### Construct: "Clockwork Without Desire"

Demons pity Constructs. A Construct does exactly what it was built to do, no more, no less. It has no hunger. It makes no choices. It cannot be Corrupted because it has nothing to spend. Demons find Constructs boring but useful — a Construct can be made to enforce a contract with perfect impartiality.

**Mechanic tie:** Demon cards that summon or interact with Constructs treat them as "neutral arbiters" — Constructs gain bonuses when a player has high Corruption because they are drawn to structure, even corrupted structure.

### Goblin: "The Scam That Works"

Demons respect Goblins more than any other faction. Goblins understand transaction better than anyone — they cheat, they bargain, they find the angle. A Goblin scam is just a Demon contract with worse handwriting. The difference is: Goblins don't honor their deals. Demons always do.

**Mechanic tie:** Goblin and Demon cards in the same deck create "shady synergy" — cards that cost less Attention when you have both high Corruption AND low HP (the desperate bargain).

### Undead: "Persistence Without Growth"

Undead endure. They don't improve. They don't learn. They just keep existing. Demons find this horrifying — an eternity without choice is worse than any hell. The Undead cannot gain Corruption (they have no soul to spend), which makes them immune to Demon traps but also unable to benefit from Demon power.

**Mechanic tie:** Undead enemies in The Pact Chambers cannot be bargained with. They must be destroyed or bypassed.

### Elemental: "Force Without Negotiation"

Elementals are pure expression — fire burns, water flows, no discussion. Demons find Elementals frustrating because they cannot make deals with a force of nature. You cannot bargain with a volcano. The only option is containment or redirection.

**Mechanic tie:** Elemental cards that deal area damage are the best counter to Demon swarms (multiple weak summons that trade in bulk).

### Aberration: "The Question That Eats"

Aberrations are the only faction Demons truly fear. An Aberration does not want power or agreement or structure. It wants to **understand** — and understanding dissolves the very thing understood. A Demon contract analyzed by an Aberration becomes meaningless, its terms reduced to noise. The Pact Chambers have a single room sealed with seven locks: the Aberration Containment Cell.

**Mechanic tie:** Aberration cards that reveal or examine cards counter Demon traps (Apocalypse Rune disarmed by "looking" at it first).

### Dragon: "The Hoard That Remembers"

Dragons and Demons are ancient rivals. Both accumulate. Both demand price. But Dragons hoard *things* — gold, memory, time. Demons hoard *choices* — contracts, Corruption, souls rendered into currency. A Dragon's treasure can be stolen. A Demon's contract is binding even if the paper burns.

**Mechanic tie:** Dragon cards that generate Quiddity counter Demon Corruption — Quiddity is "clean" currency, Corruption is "debt" currency. A deck with both must balance two economies.

### Universal: "The Currency That Transcends"

Universal cards are the loophole in every Demon contract. A Universal card does not belong to any faction, which means it does not recognize Demon jurisdiction. The Demon Boss cannot apply its clauses to Universal cards — they are "foreign entities" under Demon law.

**Mechanic tie:** Universal cards are the key to finding loopholes in The Pact Chambers. Every player should bring at least one Universal card to Floor 7.

## V. The Demon Aesthetic

### Visual Language
- Colors: Deep crimson, bruised purple, tarnished gold
- Textures: Wet stone, cracked leather, dried blood, wax seals
- Shapes: Contracts written in light, chains that tighten when looked at, doors that open only when you knock three times and mean it

### Sound Design
- Demon ambient: Low chanting in a language that sounds almost familiar but shifts when focused on
- Card play: The sound of a seal breaking, a quill scratching parchment, a coin dropping into an empty well
- Combat: Not roaring. *Whispering*. Demons fight quietly. They want you to hear the terms, not the thunder.

### Naming Convention
- Demon cards are named like legal documents or accusations: "Painforged" (not "Fireball"), "Dark Bargain" (not "Draw Cards"), "Apocalypse Rune" (not "Kill Spell")
- The names sound like things you sign, not things you shout

## VI. The Deep Lore: The First Contract

*(This section is optional — for players who want to read flavor text and understand the world. It does not affect mechanics.)*

Before the Cathedral existed, before the Gearworks turned, there was only the Void and the First Hunger.

The First Hunger wanted to exist but had no form. It could not touch, could not hold, could not experience. So it made an offer to the Void itself:

"Let me borrow your absence. I will fill it with desire. In return, every desire I create will leave a trace of its wanting — a residue that makes your emptiness deeper, richer, more profound."

The Void agreed. Not because it wanted richness, but because it was curious what wanting felt like.

The First Hunger became the First Demon. Its desire created matter. Its residue became Corruption — the trace of every wanting that ever was.

All Demon contracts are echoes of this first agreement. Every time a player chooses power over purity, they renew the First Contract. Every time they gain Corruption, they feed the First Hunger.

The Demons do not serve evil. They serve **the architecture of desire itself** — the beautiful, terrible structure that makes wanting possible, and therefore makes existence possible.

Without Demons, there is no choice. Without choice, there is no meaning. They are the price of free will, and they collect with absolute fairness.
